Indian business runs in two vocabularies at once: the words of the traditional bahi-khata and the language of modern accounting and GST. This glossary puts them side by side. Terms are grouped so related words sit together.

## Traditional khata words

Bahi (बही)
: A bound book or register. A bahi-khata is an account book.

Khata (खाता)
: An account, usually the page for one person or one head of expense.

Khatauni (खतौनी)
: The act of posting entries into the right khata; also a ledger in land records.

Rokad (रोकड़)
: Cash. Rokad bahi is the cash book.

Rokad baaki (रोकड़ बाकी)
: Cash balance at the end of the day.

Naam (नाम)
: The debit side of a personal account. Writing an amount "in someone's naam" means they owe it.

Jama (जमा)
: The credit side; amounts received from or credited to a person.

Udhaar (उधार)
: Credit given or taken. Udhaar diya means credit given; udhaar liya means credit taken.

Len-den (लेन-देन)
: Transactions; literally taking and giving.

Lena (लेना)
: Amount to be received. "Mujhe lena hai" means I will get money.

Dena (देना)
: Amount to be paid.

Baaki (बाकी)
: Balance remaining.

Hisaab (हिसाब)
: Account or calculation. Hisaab-kitaab means accounts in general.

Munim (मुनीम)
: A traditional accountant who kept the bahi for a merchant.

Seth (सेठ)
: A merchant or business owner.

Gaddi (गद्दी)
: The merchant's seat or firm; also the traditional office.

Aadhat (आढ़त)
: Commission agency, common in grain markets. The agent is an aadhatiya.

Dalali (दलाली)
: Brokerage.

Hundi (हुंडी)
: A traditional bill of exchange used to transfer money or give credit.

Byaj or sood (ब्याज, सूद)
: Interest. Often quoted in rupees per hundred per month.

Mool (मूल)
: Principal amount of a loan.

Kisht (किश्त)
: Instalment.

Karz (कर्ज़)
: Loan or debt.

Peshgi (पेशगी)
: Advance payment.

Bayana (बयाना)
: Earnest money paid to confirm a deal.

Tagada (तगादा)
: Reminder or demand for payment of dues.

Vasooli (वसूली)
: Recovery of dues.

Nafa or labh (नफ़ा, लाभ)
: Profit.

Nuksaan or ghaata (नुकसान, घाटा)
: Loss.

Poonji (पूंजी)
: Capital invested by the owner.

Aamdani (आमदनी)
: Income.

Kharcha (खर्चा)
: Expense. Jama-kharch means income and expenditure.

Maal (माल)
: Goods or stock.

Parchi (पर्ची)
: A slip or rough bill.

Chalan (चालान)
: Delivery note or challan accompanying goods.

Chopda pujan (चोपड़ा पूजन)
: The Diwali ritual of worshipping new account books, especially in Gujarat.

## Modern accounting terms

Ledger
: The book of accounts with one account per person, asset, liability, income or expense.

Journal or day book
: The book where transactions are first recorded in date order.

Debit and credit
: The left and right sides of an account. See our guide to [the golden rules]({{root}}learn/double-entry-golden-rules/).

Trial balance
: A list of all account balances, used to check that total debits equal total credits.

Debtors or accounts receivable
: Money customers owe you.

Creditors or accounts payable
: Money you owe suppliers.

Drawings
: Money or goods the owner takes from the business for personal use.

Depreciation
: Spreading the cost of an asset over its useful life.

Bad debt
: Money owed to you that you no longer expect to recover.

Gross profit
: Sales minus cost of goods sold.

Net profit
: Gross profit minus all running expenses.

Working capital
: Current assets minus current liabilities; the money that runs daily operations.

Reconciliation
: Matching your records with another source, such as a bank statement or a supplier's statement.

Balance confirmation
: A statement sent to a customer or supplier asking them to confirm the balance in their books.

Financial year
: The 12 months from 1 April to 31 March used for tax and accounts in India.

## GST and tax terms

GSTIN
: The 15-character GST identification number. The first two digits are the state code and characters 3 to 12 are the PAN. Check one with our [GSTIN checker]({{root}}tools/gstin-validator/).

CGST, SGST, UTGST and IGST
: Central, State, Union Territory and Integrated GST. Intra-state supplies attract CGST plus SGST or UTGST; inter-state supplies attract IGST.

HSN and SAC
: Codes that classify goods (HSN) and services (SAC) and decide the GST rate.

ITC (input tax credit)
: GST paid on purchases that a registered business can set off against GST on its sales.

Place of supply
: The location that decides whether a supply is intra-state or inter-state.

Reverse charge (RCM)
: Cases where the buyer, not the seller, pays the GST to the government.

Composition scheme
: A simplified GST scheme for small businesses that pay a flat percentage of turnover and cannot claim ITC.

Bill of supply
: The document issued instead of a tax invoice by composition dealers or for exempt supplies.

GSTR-1
: The return reporting details of outward supplies (sales).

GSTR-3B
: The monthly or quarterly summary return through which GST is paid.

GSTR-2B
: An auto-generated statement of input tax credit available, based on suppliers' filings.

QRMP
: Quarterly Return, Monthly Payment scheme for businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore.

CMP-08
: The quarterly statement-cum-challan filed by composition dealers.

E-invoice and IRN
: Reporting B2B invoices to the government portal, which returns an Invoice Reference Number and QR code.

E-way bill
: An electronic document for moving goods above the value threshold.

AATO
: Annual aggregate turnover, used to decide thresholds such as e-invoicing and HSN digits.

PAN
: Permanent Account Number for income tax.

TDS and TCS
: Tax deducted at source and tax collected at source.

Advance tax
: Income tax paid in instalments during the year instead of in one go at the end.

Udyam
: The government's MSME registration. See our [Udyam guide]({{root}}learn/udyam-msme-registration/).

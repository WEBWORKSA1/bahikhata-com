The right loan at the right time lets a business buy stock in bulk, serve a bigger customer or open a second counter. The wrong loan becomes an EMI that eats the profit it was meant to create. This guide covers the main options for small businesses in India and how to prepare.

Schemes and limits change. Check current terms with the lender and on official government websites before applying.

## Match the loan to the need

| Need | Better loan type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Stock, udhaar, day-to-day cash | Cash credit or overdraft limit | Interest only on what you use; repay and redraw |
| Machine, vehicle, shop fit-out | Term loan | Fixed EMI over the asset's useful life |
| Short gap before a big payment arrives | Invoice or bill discounting | Borrow against a confirmed receivable |
| Very small first loan | Mudra Shishu | Small ticket, simple documentation |

Using a long term loan to fund stock, or an overdraft to buy a machine, is a common and expensive mismatch.

## PM Mudra Yojana

Mudra loans are for non-farm income-generating micro enterprises in manufacturing, trading and services, given through banks, NBFCs and microfinance institutions. Categories:

| Category | Loan amount |
|---|---|
| Shishu | Up to ₹50,000 |
| Kishore | Above ₹50,000 up to ₹5 lakh |
| Tarun | Above ₹5 lakh up to ₹10 lakh |
| Tarun Plus | Above ₹10 lakh up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who have repaid an earlier Tarun loan |

Mudra itself is not a lender; you apply at a bank or other lending institution. Interest rates are set by the lender. Beware of agents promising "guaranteed Mudra loans" for a fee.

## Collateral-free loans under CGTMSE

The Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises guarantees eligible loans to micro and small enterprises, so lenders can lend without collateral or third-party guarantee. The guarantee ceiling was raised to ₹10 crore from April 2025. A guarantee fee applies, usually paid by the borrower. Ask your bank whether your loan can be covered.

## What lenders look at

1. **Bank statements for 6 to 12 months.** Regular credits, average balance, and no cheque bounces.
2. **Income-tax returns for 2 to 3 years.** Lenders size loans on declared income, so under-reporting income limits what you can borrow.
3. **GST returns**, if registered. Turnover in GSTR-3B should be consistent with bank credits.
4. **Credit score** of the owner and the business. Many lenders prefer a personal score above about 700. Pay every EMI and credit card bill on time.
5. **Business proof.** Udyam registration, GST certificate, shop licence, rent agreement.
6. **Existing EMIs.** Most lenders want total EMIs to stay well within your monthly surplus.

Clean, regular books make every one of these easier. A [digital khata]({{root}}khata/) and a cash book that tallies are the foundation.

## Check affordability before you apply

Work out the EMI with our [EMI calculator]({{root}}tools/emi-calculator/). Then ask:

- Will the extra profit from this loan comfortably exceed the EMI?
- Could I still pay the EMI if sales dropped by 20% for three months?
- What is the total interest over the full tenure?

If the business cannot cover the EMI from its own cash flow, the loan is too big or too short.

## Red flags

- Any upfront "processing" payment to an individual before sanction.
- Lenders not registered with the RBI, or apps asking for access to your contacts and photos.
- Interest quoted only "per month" or as a flat rate. A 2% per month flat rate is far more than 24% a year on a reducing balance.

For generations, many Indian traders closed their old bahi and opened a new one at Diwali. The new book was blessed, the first entry was written with care, and the year's accounts started clean. Today the tax year runs from 1 April to 31 March, but the spirit of the ritual is still the best way to run a business: close properly, carry forward only what is real, and start fresh.

## Two calendars, one business

- **The traditional year** in many trading communities, especially in Gujarat, starts the day after Diwali. Chopda pujan marks the new books.
- **The financial year** for tax, GST and statutory accounts runs from 1 April to 31 March.

Many family businesses use both: a Diwali-to-Diwali internal view and an April-to-March set of accounts for tax. If you do this, make sure the April-to-March accounts are complete in their own right.

## The closing checklist

Work through this in the last two weeks of March, or at whatever date you close.

### 1. Customers and suppliers

- Send each major customer and supplier a statement and ask them to confirm the balance.
- Investigate any difference: usually a payment one side has not recorded or a bill one side has not received.
- Decide which old balances are unrecoverable and write them off as bad debts.

The [digital khata]({{root}}khata/) can share a full statement for any party in one tap.

### 2. Cash and bank

- Count cash in hand on the closing date and match it with the cash book.
- Reconcile every bank account with the bank statement, including cheques issued but not yet cleared.
- Record bank charges and interest that appear only on the statement.

### 3. Stock

- Do a physical count on the closing date.
- Value stock at cost or market value, whichever is lower, using the same method as last year.
- Separate damaged, expired and dead stock. Our [stock register guide]({{root}}learn/stock-register-inventory/) explains the process.

### 4. Expenses and income

- Record bills that relate to this year but have not been paid, such as March electricity.
- Separate expenses paid in advance for next year, such as an annual software subscription.
- Record depreciation on equipment.
- Separate the owner's personal expenses from business expenses.

### 5. GST and tax

- Match your sales register with GSTR-1 and your purchase register with GSTR-2B.
- Check that input tax credit claimed matches eligible invoices.
- Pay the final advance tax instalment by 15 March if you are liable.
- Note the upcoming deadlines for returns in our [due-date calendar]({{root}}tools/due-date-calendar/).

### 6. Carry forward

- Carry forward only verified balances: confirmed customer and supplier balances, counted cash, reconciled bank and counted stock.
- Keep the closed year's books, bills and backups safe. Tax law generally requires books to be preserved for several years.
- In a digital khata, download a backup labelled with the year before starting a new book.

## Starting the new bahi

Whether it is Diwali or 1 April, the first page of a new book should contain:

1. Opening cash and bank balances.
2. Opening balance for each customer and supplier.
3. Opening stock value.
4. Any loans outstanding.

A clean opening is worth more than any software. Every error not fixed at closing is carried into next year and becomes harder to find.

## Common questions
